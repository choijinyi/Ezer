The purpose of this writing is to study how *soccer mission activity* as *Sports* culture influences the church, and to evaluate the positive factors of **soccer mission activity**.

The seven **Research Questions** of this study are as follows:

1. As a *Sports* culture, does **soccer mission activity** strengthen the intimacy of the community?
2. As a *Sports* culture, how does **soccer mission activity** influence evangelism, service, and volunteer work?
3. As a *Sports* culture, does **soccer mission activity** have a connecting function between effective evangelism and mission?
4. As a *Sports* culture, does **soccer mission activity** strengthen mission strategy for the next generation?
5. As a *Sports* culture, does **soccer mission activity** influence the relationship with the senior pastor?
6. As a *Sports* culture, does **soccer mission activity** help spiritual maturity?
7. As a *Sports* culture, does **soccer mission activity** provide psychological stability?

These questions are considered in order to contribute to the church-restoration movement. By minimizing a variety of problems and applying meaningful and beneficial findings to the church, this study aims to advance the recovery of the local congregation.

The Korean church went through a tremendous environmental change in or around 2003. The number of Christians decreased from 12,825,001 in 2003 to 8,616,438 in 2005 — a decrease of 4,200,000 in two years. After ten years, in 2015, the total was counted at 9,675,761.

The five-day work week, which led many believers to leave the church, and a dearth of appropriate policies for children and youth are among the principal causes.

In addition, the *COVID-19 Pandemic*, which originated in Wuhan, China, in December 2019, paralyzed society and the economy for three years and caused serious damage to relational networks. All of these factors put a damper on the church, which had just been sprouting on the way to recovery.

This researcher pondered deeply how, within a church that has survived such a turbulent environment, children, youth, college and young adults, and older adults might be united together. Spreading the gospel through *culture* is one of the possible solutions this research has identified. That solution is **soccer mission activity** as *Sports* culture.

The research is centered upon seven *RQs* and yields meaningful results. As a *Sports* culture applicable to the situation of 2023, the gospel and the mission activity that carries its worth can be placed upon the wagon of soccer, and *Seo-bu Church* is expected to carry it to the entire world.

According to the results of this research, **soccer mission activity** as *Sports* culture demonstrates several meaningful effects:

1. Strengthening the intimacy of relationships within the community
2. Contributing to evangelism, volunteer work, and service
3. Bringing together effective evangelism and mission
4. Influencing the relationship with the senior pastor
5. Helping spiritual maturity and providing psychological stability

These findings were consistently confirmed through both **quantitative** and **qualitative** data.

> Through the interviews, soccer is shown to be one of the most effective means for spreading the gospel through *person-to-person* encounter and the forging of relationships.

Regardless of class or generation, **soccer mission activity** enables believers to maintain bonds within the church and provides opportunities for soccer-club members and their families to meet one another.

Now, more than twenty years after 2003, it is hoped that **soccer mission activity** as *Sports* culture will be applied still more widely, so that the Korean church may once again turn toward a season of revival.
